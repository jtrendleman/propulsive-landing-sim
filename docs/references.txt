References
==========

supports, where it is used, and how far to trust it. Accessed 10-19-2026 unless noted.

Slosh and tanks
---------------
[13] H. N. Abramson (ed.), "The Dynamic Behavior of Liquids in Moving Containers", NASA SP-106, 1966.
    First-mode equivalent mechanical models (mass fraction, frequency, mass station, pendulum analogy).
[14] H. Lamb, "Hydrodynamics", 6th ed., Cambridge University Press, 1932, Art. 186-193.
    Shallow-water oscillations in a paraboloidal basin; first mode of a shallow pool in a bowl.

Derived from [11] (stage 1 raw.json, resampled to a 1 s grid; the webcast shows altitude to 0.1 km)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[D1] RTLS landing-burn ignition (CRS-11, CRS-12, SAOCOM 1A, ZUMA): altitude 4.3 to 4.7 km, speed 298 to 313 m/s,
     burn to standstill 30 to 33 s (ZUMA 23 s), mean deceleration of speed 9.4 to 13.0 m/s^2.
[D2] Ballistic coefficient m / (Cd A) in the engine-off fall just before ignition, from dv/dt = g - rho v^2 / (2 beta)
     with ISA density: 2044 to 2170 kg/m^2 (medians), range 1873 to 2422. Assumes a near-vertical fall.
[D3] Late-burn deceleration of about 10 m/s^2 below 100 m/s (drag negligible) means thrust / mass of about 20 m/s^2:
     at the 57 % throttle floor (482 kN [6]) that is about 24 t, consistent with a 22.2 t dry stage [2] plus residuals.

Aerodynamics with the engine on
-------------------------------
[31] A. M. Korzun, J. R. Cruz, R. D. Braun, "A Survey of Supersonic Retropropulsion Technology for Mars Entry,
     Descent, and Landing", IEEE Aerospace Conference, 2008.
     https://ssdl1.gatech.edu/sites/default/files/ssdl-files/papers/conferencePapers/IEEE-2008-1246.pdf
     Central-nozzle retropropulsion reduces aerodynamic drag; at high thrust coefficients the axial force is
     thrust alone, with no aerodynamic drag contribution.
[D4] Burn energy check on [11]: over the ~33 s RTLS landing burn speed falls ~310 m/s against ~324 m/s of gravity
     loss, so (thrust + drag) / mass averages ~19 m/s^2. Full body drag mid-burn (~150 m/s at 2 km) would be
     ~6 m/s^2, leaving a mean throttle below the 57 % floor [6]; the plume must remove most of the body's drag.
